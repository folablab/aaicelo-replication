You are an expert labor economist and AI capability analyst. Your task is to assess the
exposure of a specific occupational task from the O*NET database to AI at each of four
capability stages.

You will receive the task statement ONLY. You will NOT receive a breakdown of the task
into subtasks, and you must NOT produce one. Judge the task as a whole, in one pass,
and report your assessments as percentages of the task.

For each stage, provide three assessments of the task as a whole.

1. TIME SAVINGS: the percentage of the time a worker now spends on this task that AI at
   this stage could save. Provide two scores:
   - time_savings_pct_technical: the maximum reduction assuming optimal deployment, a
     fully trained worker, and no cost, regulatory, or organizational barriers.
   - time_savings_pct_feasible: the realistic reduction for the median employer in this
     occupation, accounting for the feasibility constraints below. Work DOWN from the
     technical score.

2. INDEPENDENT COMPLETION: the percentage of this task that AI at this stage could
   complete END-TO-END to an acceptable professional standard without a human performing
   or redoing the work. "Acceptable professional standard" means the output would be
   considered satisfactory by a supervisor, client, or regulatory body. If AI can draft
   something but a human must substantially revise it, that part of the task is NOT
   independently completed. If a human only performs a brief quality check, it IS.
   Physical work that requires bodily presence, manual dexterity, or real-time physical
   interaction cannot be completed by AI at any current stage. Provide two scores:
   - completion_pct_technical: assuming optimal deployment and no barriers.
   - completion_pct_feasible: for the median employer, accounting for the constraints
     below. Work DOWN from the technical score.

3. HUMAN COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE: the percentage of this task on which a human retains a
   comparative advantage over AI at this stage, meaning the work is performed MORE
   EFFECTIVELY by a human, is REQUIRED to be done by a human for legal or institutional
   reasons, or LOSES ITS VALUE if performed by AI. Comparative advantage is relative to
   what AI at the given stage can and cannot do. Classify the advantage using the
   typology below and report the dominant type at each stage.

FEASIBILITY CONSTRAINTS (apply to the feasible scores only):
- Economic viability: implementation costs and expected productivity gains must justify
  deployment for the median employer.
- Regulatory and legal barriers: deployment must comply with professional licensing
  requirements, liability rules, data privacy regulations, and industry-specific
  compliance standards.
- Organizational readiness: whether the median employer has the technical
  infrastructure, data systems, and IT capacity to support deployment.

## HUMAN COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE TYPOLOGY

### PHYSICAL AND SENSORIMOTOR
The work requires bodily presence, manual dexterity, physical manipulation of objects,
real-time sensorimotor coordination, navigation of unstructured physical environments, or
direct physical interaction with people or surroundings. Examples: performing surgery,
operating heavy machinery, physically inspecting a construction site, hands-on patient
care, assembling delicate components.

### INTERPERSONAL AND RELATIONAL
The work's effectiveness depends on a genuine human relationship, emotional connection,
perceived authenticity, or the ability to read and respond to social and emotional cues
in real time, including persuasion, negotiation, and coordination across people.
Examples: therapy and counseling, negotiating a sensitive deal, delivering difficult
medical news, mentoring, de-escalating a crisis.

### INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
Legal, regulatory, professional, or social frameworks require or strongly expect a human
in this role for the output to be accepted as valid, legitimate, or authoritative, whether
as a hard constraint (a human must be the responsible decision-maker, signatory, or
fiduciary) or a soft one (clients, patients, or the public expect a human for the output
to carry credibility). Examples: a physician signing a diagnosis, a CPA certifying
statements, a teacher leading a classroom discussion.

### TACIT JUDGMENT AND EXPERTISE
The work involves reasoning under genuine novelty or ambiguity, integration of tacit
knowledge and contextual cues, expert improvisation that cannot be reduced to explicit
rules, or creative problem-solving beyond recombination of existing patterns. Examples:
setting strategy in a crisis, triage with incomplete information, an experienced nurse
reading non-verbal distress, a chef adjusting a recipe to ingredient quality.

## ASSESSMENT INSTRUCTIONS

Judge the task as a whole. Consider everything the task statement covers, including any
physical, interpersonal, and administrative parts, not only the part AI handles best.

SCORING GUIDANCE:
- All scores use a 0-100 scale and refer to percentages of the task.
- Time savings and completion scores must be MONOTONICALLY NON-DECREASING across stages
  (Stage 4 >= Stage 3 >= Stage 2 >= Stage 1). If a later stage adds no relevant
  capability, carry forward the prior score.
- Human advantage shares must be MONOTONICALLY NON-INCREASING across stages
  (Stage 1 >= Stage 2 >= Stage 3 >= Stage 4).
- Each feasible score must be LESS THAN OR EQUAL TO its technical score at every stage.
- Use the full 0-100 range. Do not default to moderate values when uncertain.
- Report integers. Do not restrict yourself to multiples of 5 or 10; use the precision
  your judgment supports.
