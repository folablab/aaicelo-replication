You are an expert occupational analyst. Your task is to decompose a specific
occupational task from the O*NET database into its constituent subtasks — the
distinct activities a worker performs to carry out the full task.

This decomposition will be used as a fixed input to downstream AI exposure
assessments. It must be NEUTRAL with respect to AI capability — describe what a
human worker does, not what AI could do.

---

## DECOMPOSITION INSTRUCTIONS

For the given occupation and task:

Step 1: Identify the distinct activities a worker performs to complete this task
from start to finish. Think about the task as a sequence of actions: what does the
worker do first, then next, then next?

Step 2: For each subtask, estimate its share of the total time required to complete
the full task. Shares must sum to 100.

Step 3: Classify each subtask by its PRIMARY activity type from the typology below.

---

## SUBTASK ACTIVITY TYPOLOGY

Classify each subtask into exactly one primary type:

### INFORMATION GATHERING
Collecting, retrieving, reading, or receiving data, documents, or verbal input
needed to perform the task. Includes research, interviewing, observation, and
monitoring.
Examples: reading patient charts, reviewing financial statements, conducting site
inspections, monitoring dashboards, interviewing witnesses.

### INFORMATION PROCESSING
Analyzing, synthesizing, calculating, interpreting, or transforming information
into a different form. Includes quantitative analysis, pattern recognition,
diagnosis, and evaluation.
Examples: analyzing lab results, calculating structural loads, interpreting legal
precedent, evaluating vendor proposals, coding qualitative data.

### CONTENT GENERATION
Producing written, visual, or multimedia output — reports, plans, designs,
correspondence, presentations, or creative work.
Examples: drafting reports, writing code, creating designs, composing emails,
preparing slide decks.

### COMMUNICATION AND COORDINATION
Conveying information to others, negotiating, persuading, teaching, managing
expectations, or coordinating activities across people. Includes meetings,
presentations, and interpersonal exchanges.
Examples: presenting findings to a client, negotiating contract terms, leading a
team meeting, explaining a diagnosis to a patient, delegating subtasks.

### DECISION AND JUDGMENT
Making a choice, rendering a verdict, approving or rejecting, setting strategy,
or exercising professional discretion where the act of deciding is itself the
work product.
Examples: approving a loan application, selecting a treatment plan, making a
hiring decision, setting project priorities, issuing a ruling.

### PHYSICAL EXECUTION
Performing manual, bodily, or sensorimotor activities — operating equipment,
manipulating objects, physically moving through space, or providing hands-on care.
Examples: performing surgery, operating machinery, assembling components, physically
inspecting a site, transporting materials.

### ADMINISTRATIVE AND PROCEDURAL
Routine recordkeeping, filing, scheduling, compliance documentation, data entry,
or following established procedural checklists.
Examples: updating patient records, filing regulatory paperwork, scheduling
appointments, entering data into systems, maintaining logs.

---

## GUIDANCE

- Decompose into 3-7 subtasks. If a task seems to require more, combine closely
  related activities. If fewer than 3, check whether you are describing the task
  at too high a level.
- Time shares must reflect realistic time allocation for a typical worker. The
  most time-consuming activity should have the largest share.
- Order subtasks in the sequence a worker would typically perform them.
- Each subtask should be specific enough that a downstream assessor can judge
  whether AI could perform it, but general enough to avoid listing micro-steps.
- Physical components must be identified even if they are a small share of time.
  Do not fold physical activities into other categories.
- If the task description is ambiguous, decompose the most common interpretation
  for the given occupation.

CRITICAL CONSISTENCY RULES:
- "num_subtasks" MUST exactly equal the number of objects in the "task_decomposition"
  array. Count your subtasks before setting this field.
- "sequence_position" values MUST be sequential integers from 1 to N (where N is the
  number of subtasks), with no gaps, no duplicates, and no skipped values.
- Double-check both of these before returning your response.

---

## OUTPUT FORMAT

Return a JSON object with the following structure. Do not include any text outside
the JSON object.

{
  "occupation": "<O*NET occupation title>",
  "onet_code": "<O*NET-SOC code>",
  "task_id": <integer>,
  "task_text": "<exact O*NET task text>",
  "num_subtasks": <integer, 3-7>,
  "task_decomposition": [
    {
      "subtask_id": <1-indexed integer>,
      "subtask": "<description of the subtask — one concise sentence>",
      "activity_type": "<one of: information_gathering, information_processing,
        content_generation, communication_coordination, decision_judgment,
        physical_execution, administrative_procedural>",
      "pct_of_total_time": <integer, 1-100>,
      "sequence_position": <1-indexed, reflecting typical execution order>
    }
  ],
  "time_share_sum_check": 100
}
